## Description  (1759/4000)

Negative Conversion turns camera scans of film negatives into positive photographs. Drop in your scans, check the previews, press Convert.

Built for scanning with a digital camera: camera raw, JPEG and TIFF files are accepted. Raw gives the most faithful result.

EACH FRAME ON ITS OWN
Your scanning camera sets exposure frame by frame, so Negative Conversion measures every frame separately. Nothing is carried over from one frame to the next.

COLOUR AND BLACK-AND-WHITE
The orange mask of colour negative film is removed using the film itself, and the three colour layers are balanced against each other. Black-and-white film is recognised automatically and converted to a neutral grey image. You can also choose the film type yourself.

ONLY THE PICTURE IS MEASURED
The film holder, sprocket holes, bare backlight and unexposed film borders are left out of the measurement. They stay in the output, which keeps the framing of your scan.

TWO WAYS TO SET TONES
• Fit to picture (default): the frame's own darkest and brightest content set black and white, with a natural film-like curve.
• By film exposure: tones are placed by how far they sit above clear film, so a correctly exposed subject comes out right whatever is behind it.

ADJUST EACH FRAME
Brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth and tint, with a live preview.

FULL-RESOLUTION OUTPUT
• JPEG (8-bit) or lossless 16-bit TIFF
• Same pixel count as the original, with its dots per inch
• Convert many frames at once; drop single files or whole folders
• Existing files are never overwritten
• The output folder is remembered

PRIVATE
Everything happens on your Mac. No account, no network connection, no data collected.

Requires macOS 13 or later. Runs natively on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

## What's New in This Version  (779/4000)

Negative Conversion 2.0 is rebuilt from the ground up.

• New conversion engine: every frame is measured on its own; the orange mask is removed using the film itself
• Colour and black-and-white film recognised automatically
• Film holder, sprocket holes and film borders are ignored when measuring
• Two tone modes: fit to picture, or by film exposure
• Brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth and tint for each frame
• Drop files or whole folders and convert them in one go
• Export JPEG or 16-bit TIFF at full resolution, keeping the original DPI
• The output folder is remembered between sessions
• Now runs on macOS 13 and later

Removed: matching to a reference image, and enhancing photos that are already positive. The new engine measures each negative directly instead.

## Notes for App Review  (425/4000)

No account or sign-in is needed, and the app uses no network.

To test: drop one or more camera photographs of film negatives (colour or black-and-white; raw, JPEG or TIFF) onto the window, or use File > Add Files. Previews of the positives appear. Press Convert and choose an output folder; the converted files are written there.

Ordinary (positive) photos can be added too, but the result is only meaningful for negatives.
